1 — The waiting list

Ten cars and a good week is all it takes for the fleet to be empty. The honest answer then is "nothing today", and a visitor who reads that and closes the tab is lost. This turns that moment into an email address.

What the customer sees

Nothing at all, on any normal day. The notice appears only when the system knows the fleet is empty, and it appears in the same breath as the bad news rather than as a second thing to find:

On screen

No cars are available right now
Leave your email address and we will write to you as soon as one is free.

[ you@example.com ]  [ Notify me ]

It is in German and English, following whatever language the visitor is already reading. On /book it sits above the wizard, so it is read before somebody fills in several screens of dates and licence details to reach an answer of "nothing today" — but the form below still works, because a booking is a WhatsApp conversation with you and you may well still want it. On /pickup it replaces the car picker entirely, because an open dropdown with no options tells a customer standing at your desk nothing.

One field, and no others

The form asks for an email address and nothing else. No name, no telephone number, no preferred model. Every extra field is one more thing held about somebody who is not yet a customer, and the only thing needed to write to them is the address. Asking twice is treated as asking once — the same address never creates a second row, so a double-click cannot produce two emails.

How the notification actually happens

This is the part worth understanding, because it is the part that decides whether anybody ever gets told.

A car becoming free is not an event this system can watch. A rental ends because somebody presses "close"; a car leaves the garage because somebody edits it; a new car arrives through the fleet form. Three unrelated paths — and wiring a "send the emails now" call into each one means the fourth path, added next year, silently sends nothing.

So the daily run asks the only question that is always answerable instead: is anything available right now, and is anybody still waiting to hear it? That stays correct however the car became free, including by a route nobody has thought of yet. The practical consequence is the honest one:

Timing

Somebody who signs up is written to on the next daily run after a car is free — not within seconds. Given that the email says "availability changes through the day, get in touch soon", this is the right trade. If you ever want it immediate, the pass is a single function and can be called from the close-rental button as well.

The email

Short, in the language the person signed up in, and it links straight at the booking page. It deliberately does not name a plate: the car that is free at nine may be let by lunchtime, and a mail promising ZH 615 132 specifically is a promise the system cannot keep.

Every message carries a visible unsubscribe link. That link opens a page with a confirm button rather than unsubscribing on the click itself — mail scanners and corporate antivirus fetch every link in a message to check it, and without the extra step some people would be removed from the list by their own IT department, having never clicked anything. That failure ("I asked to be told and never heard from you") is one nobody would ever diagnose.

Nobody is written to twice about the same wait. Once the message has gone the row is marked, so a fleet with one idle car does not mail the whole list every morning.

What you see

The fleet screen's heading carries the count when it is not zero — for example Vehicles · 10 · 0 available · 5 people waiting for a car. It is demand you can act on: five people waiting is the argument for getting a car out of the garage today rather than on Friday. On almost every day it is zero, and the heading stays quiet.

2 — Car photographs

You said the photographs were coming and asked us to be ready for them. Everything around them is built: upload, storage, delivery and display. Adding a picture is now a thirty-second job per car, with no deploy.

Why the dropdown had to be replaced

A native dropdown cannot contain an image. An <option> may hold text and nothing else, in every browser, and no amount of styling changes that. Showing a car beside its plate meant building the list by hand — which is not a decision taken lightly, because the native one is keyboard-accessible, screen-reader-correct and renders as the phone's own wheel for free. All of that was rebuilt deliberately: arrow keys, Home and End, Enter, Escape and screen-reader announcements all work.

Having built it, the photograph is shown four ways rather than only on hover, because hovering does not exist on the phone your staff actually use at the kerb:

WhereWhatWhy
Large preview, above the listFollows whichever row is highlighted Hovering with a mouse and arrowing with a keyboard highlight the same thing, so the feature works without a pointer
Thumbnail on every rowSmall photo beside the model and plate Works on a touchscreen, where nothing can be hovered
Under the closed fieldThe chosen car, full width The confirmation of what was picked is the car itself, not a plate to be recognised
Return form tooThe same picker A customer handing back one of six identical Priuses picks the right row by looking at it, not by reading six plates that differ in one digit

Adding a photograph

Fleet screen → the ⋮ menu at the end of a car's row → Edit → Vehicle photo → Choose a photo. That is all; it is live immediately on the booking and return forms, with no deploy and no developer. The photograph sits with the model, the plate and the chassis number because it is part of what the car is, not a record of work done to it.

DetailValue
Accepted formatsJPEG, PNG, WebP
Maximum size2 MB, after the browser shrinks it
ResizingAutomatic — scaled to 1600px and re-encoded before upload
Shape on screenCropped to a wide landscape box, so a photo taken side-on works best

A photograph straight off a phone is three to six megabytes, and the hosting platform refuses a request that large — so the shrinking is not a nicety. Without it the upload would simply fail on the device you would actually use. HEIC, which is what an iPhone produces by default, is converted by the browser on the way through, so it is not something you have to think about.

Before the photographs arrive

A car with no photograph is not a broken state and does not look like one. The row shows a car silhouette and the preview says "No photo yet" in words. Most of the fleet will be in exactly that condition for a while, and the picker reads as deliberate throughout — so you can add the ten photographs one at a time, in any order, whenever they come in.

Caching, and why a replacement shows up at once

Each photograph's address carries a version stamp, so browsers are told to keep it for a year and the preview appears instantly rather than reloading on every hover. When you replace a picture the address changes, so the new one appears immediately. You will never be in the position of having replaced a photo and being told it did not work.

One thing to know about privacy

Car photographs are served to anybody who asks, unlike the identity documents, which stay behind a login and are logged on every read. That is deliberate: the pickup form is filled in by a customer who is signed into nothing, and a picture of a car parked outside your office is the same photograph you would put on the front page. Do not photograph a car with a person, a number plate of a customer's own vehicle, or paperwork visible in the shot.

3 — The maintenance record

Your sketch showed five figures pinned above the fleet table and two lines of repairs pinned below it. Everything in it is there, and the repairs became something rather better than two text boxes — the reasoning is below.

The fleet table now

Three columns were added, between Status and MFK. Each one shows the figure and, underneath it, the thing you would otherwise have to work out yourself:

ColumnShowsAnd underneath
Mileage100'000 km the date it was read — because 100'000 km read this morning and 100'000 km read in March are different facts about the same car
Service107'000 km "in 7'000 km", or a red "Service overdue" once the reading is passed
Repairs2 planned nothing when there is none — an amber chip only when something is owed
MFK existing30.11.2026 unchanged: "MFK due" two days out, "MFK overdue" after

Where the figures are entered

The ⋮ menu at the end of every row has a Maintenance entry, and it opens one dialog holding the service figures and the repairs. It is a second dialog rather than more fields in the existing edit box, because the two are opened for different reasons: the edit box is for correcting a plate, done once in a car's life, and this one is opened after every service, after every garage visit and every time somebody reads an odometer. Mixing them would mean the frequent job scrolling past the rare one.

It also stays open after each save, so you can enter a mileage, tick off a repair and add another without reopening it three times.

Why a service is counted in kilometres, not months

A garage schedules on distance, and the odometer is what your staff can read. Deriving a date from an assumed monthly mileage would be wrong in both directions at once: a car that spent a month on the forecourt would be called in early, and a taxi doing 4'000 km a month would sail straight past its interval. So you type the due reading off the garage's sticker, and the system counts down to it. It never invents an interval that could quietly disagree with your windscreen.

The system stays silent unless it can be sure

A warning appears only when both the current reading and the due reading are recorded. One without the other says nothing about whether a car needs a service today, and a screen that guessed would be believed exactly once. A blank column means "nobody has told us", never "this car is fine".

The warning starts 1'000 km before the due reading — roughly a fortnight for a car in daily service, which is long enough to get a garage slot and short enough that the warning still means something when it appears.

A reading is dated by the server, and only when it changes

The "read on" date is stamped by the system, never sent by the browser, because the whole value of that date is that it says when somebody actually looked at a dashboard. And it moves only when the number moves: the dialog saves every field it holds, so re-opening it to correct an MFK date would otherwise re-date an untouched odometer reading to today. That is precisely the lie the column exists to prevent, and there is a test asserting it.

Repairs — why not two text boxes

Your sketch showed Repairs_done: bumpers, back right wheel replaced and Repairs-planned: windshield. Two text boxes would have been the smaller change and the wrong one. They cannot say when something was done, cannot be sorted, and lose the previous entry every time somebody types over them — and a car's repair history is the first thing an insurer, a buyer or a damage dispute asks for.

So each repair is its own entry, and planned and done are one lifecycle rather than two separate lists. Ticking off a planned repair keeps the line that recorded it being planned, so you can later see that the windscreen was flagged in September and replaced in November.

Each repair holdsNotes
What needs doingFree text, up to 500 characters. No dropdown of repair types — a list like that is wrong within a week and nobody maintains it
Planned for / done onA date. Ticking "done" without naming a day dates it today; putting it back to planned clears the date again
OdometerOptional — places the repair against the service history, not only against the calendar
Cost in CHFOptional. Left empty when the invoice has not arrived; a zero would claim the repair was free
Who recorded itFilled in automatically from the signed-in account, and it outlives that account being disabled

Outstanding work is listed above the history and never mixed into it: "what still has to happen to this car" is the question the list is opened to answer. A repair entered by mistake can be deleted outright — nothing else in the system points at it, and the common case is a line typed against the wrong vehicle.

Recording something already finished is a single checkbox on the add form, for the ordinary case of typing up a garage invoice after the fact.

One pre-existing bug fixed along the way

The pickup form treated an empty fleet response as a failed request and fell back to the ten cars compiled into the source — so when every car was genuinely out, it offered all ten as available. That is the one case where the fallback actively lied, and it had to be fixed before "no cars available" could ever be shown truthfully. A failed request still falls back, as it should; an empty answer is now believed.
