The Fleet Row Controls

Every button at the end of a car's row: what it does, when it appears, whether it acts immediately or opens a dialog, and what to expect afterwards. Two of the six are new; the rest were always there and are documented because nothing ever wrote them down.

The row at a glance

One button at the end of every row — ⋮, three dots. Click it and everything you can do to that car opens as a short list, in words.

What is in the menu

⏱  Maintenance
✎  Edit
────────────
🔧  To the garage
⊘  Take off the road
────────────
🗑  Delete

Above the first line — nothing happens to the car. Both entries open a dialog. You can close it and nothing has changed.

Below it — one click changes the car. No dialog, no second chance (except delete, which asks). To the garage takes a vehicle out of the booking pool the instant you press it.

This used to be five separate buttons laid along the row. Two problems with that, both now gone. They were grey glyphs you had to learn — the wrench sat beside the gauge, their tooltips read To the garage and Maintenance, two things that sound identical and do completely different jobs. And they were wide: about 300 px of the row, which pushed the table past the width of the page and made the whole thing scroll sideways. The part that scrolled out of sight was the buttons.

The table no longer scrolls sideways

On a desktop screen the fleet table now fits the page as it is. You will still get a sideways scroll on a phone, where eight columns of vehicle data genuinely cannot fit — that is the honest trade against squeezing the plate until it wraps.

Which entries appear, and when

The menu does not always hold all five. An action that cannot legally be taken is removed rather than greyed out, so you are never left wondering why a line does nothing. The dividing rules disappear with the entries they were separating.

Why a rented car has almost no actions

A car is Rented out because a signed contract says somebody is driving it. Freeing it from this screen would leave a live rental pointing at a car the booking form is simultaneously offering to the next customer. It is released by closing its rental under Rentals — never from here.

1 — Maintenance

Opens the maintenance record. New. The one you will use most.

SectionFieldsSaves
Service Mileage · Next service at · Last service at · Service date On Save
Repairs Planned on top, Done below. Each: description, date, mileage, cost, who recorded it Each action immediately

What to expect

  • A grey summary box shows Read (when the odometer was last read) and the live countdown — in 7'000 km, or red overdue by 1'200 km. It updates as you type, so you can see the warning you are about to create before saving.
  • The dialog stays open after each save. You usually do three things in one visit; closing on the first would mean reopening it three times.
  • The ✓ on a planned repair marks it done and dates it today. The ↺ sends it back to planned and clears that date.
  • 🗑 on a repair asks once, then deletes it outright. Nothing else in the system points at a repair, so there is nothing to orphan.
The rule that keeps the figures honest

A service warning appears only when both the current reading and the due reading are filled in. One without the other says nothing about whether a car needs a service, so the system stays silent rather than guessing. A blank column means "nobody has told us" — never "this car is fine".

The Read date is stamped by the system and only moves when the number actually changes. Re-opening the dialog to fix something else will not re-date an untouched odometer reading to today.

2 — Edit

Opens the car's identity. Rarely needed — typically once, when a plate was mistyped.

FieldNotes
Vehicle photo JPEG, PNG or WebP. Shrunk automatically before upload. Saves instantly — it does not wait for Save.
Make and modelRequired
PlateRequired. Must be unique across the fleet
Chassis no.Optional. Printed on the contract when present
Next MFKOptional. Drives the MFK warning and the email

What to expect

  • Everything except the photo is written only when you press Save. Closing on Cancel discards typed changes — but not a photo you replaced, which is already saved. The dialog says so under the buttons.
  • Entering a plate another car already has is refused with "That plate is already registered.". The dialog stays open with your text intact so you can fix one character.
  • Correcting a plate does not break existing contracts. The car keeps its internal identifier; only what is printed changes.
Two things that were wrong here, now fixed

The pencil's tooltip used to read Save — it was reusing the Save label. Screen readers announced it as a save button, telling a blind user that pressing it would commit something. It now reads Edit.

The photo used to live in the maintenance dialog. A photograph is not a service record — it is what the car is, like its plate — so it moved here.

3 — To the garage / Back on the road

Toggles the car between Available and Garage / MFK. Immediate, no confirmation.

What to expect

  • The car disappears from the booking and pickup forms at once. A customer cannot select it, and no contract can name it.
  • Press it again to bring the car back. The label and tooltip flip to Back on the road.
  • It changes status only. It records nothing about what is being done — that is what the gauge is for. Sending a car to the garage and noting why are two separate actions, deliberately.
The system uses this button too

Two days before a car's MFK falls due, the nightly run moves it to Garage / MFK by itself and emails the office. That is why the wrench also works in reverse — without it, a car the system blocked could never be freed from this screen. After the inspection: enter the new MFK date with the pencil, then press the wrench to put it back on the road.

4 — Take off the road / Put back on the road

Retires a car, or brings a retired one back. Immediate, no confirmation. The heavier of the two status changes, and the one you should reach for least often.

Off the road versus Garage — what is the difference?

Garage / MFKTake off the road
MeaningTemporarily off the roadNo longer part of the working fleet
BookableNoNo
Set by the systemYes, for MFKNever — only by you
Typical useService, repair, inspectionSold, written off, returned to the lessor

Both hide the car from customers. The difference is what you are telling your colleagues — and the MFK pass respects it: it will not touch a car you have deliberately retired.

This is the safe alternative to deleting

A car with rental history cannot be deleted, and should not be. Retiring it removes it from every customer-facing list while keeping every contract, every invoice and the traffic-fine lookup intact.

5 — Delete

Removes the car permanently. Asks once. Works only in one narrow case.

What to expect

  • The menu swaps Delete for two new lines, Delete this car? in red and Cancel underneath it. Nothing is deleted until you press the red one, and closing the menu cancels it.
  • It is refused for any car that has ever been rented. You will see: "This car has rental history and cannot be deleted. Take it off the road instead."
  • So in practice it is only for a car somebody has just added by mistake — a mistyped plate entered twice, for example.
Why deletion is deliberately this hard

Every rental and every signed contract points at the car's row. Deleting one would break the "who was driving ZH 589 864 on the 12th" lookup you need when a traffic fine arrives weeks later, and would orphan signed documents held under a ten-year retention obligation.

Take off the road is the delete that is safe, and it is what the refusal message points you at.

What the row shows you before you click anything

Three columns were added alongside these controls. Each shows a figure and, underneath, the thing you would otherwise have to work out.

A dash means nothing has been recorded. It never means the car is fine.

Changed in this round

1

Five buttons became one three-dot menu

The buttons took about 300 px at the end of every row, which pushed the fleet table wider than the page and made it scroll sideways — hiding the very buttons you were reaching for. The table now fits a desktop screen, and each action is named in words instead of being a grey glyph to memorise.

fixes the sideways scroll
2

Deleting can no longer move under your pointer

Pressing delete used to swap one icon for two much wider buttons, pushing the red confirm onto a second line — a destructive control arriving under the pointer at the moment of clicking. The confirmation is now a second row of the menu, and closing the menu cancels it.

was a real hazard
3

The English labels say MFK, not "inspection"

Translating MFK into "next inspection" invented a second thing to wonder about, sitting as it does one dialog away from a service book. MFK is the word on the certificate and the word the garage uses, so the English column now uses it too.

wording
4

The repair form fits inside its dialog again

Cost CHF was being pushed through the right-hand edge of the maintenance dialog, because three columns plus a date field asked for more width than the dialog has. Two columns now, and the cost box sits on its own line.

layout bug
5

The photo moved from Maintenance to Edit

A photograph is identity, not a maintenance record. The maintenance dialog is now only Service and Repairs, which makes the dialog you open weekly noticeably shorter.
