What it costs you
  • Returns to confirm and Mail unsent — the only two numbers that mean somebody must act — sit fifth and seventh in a row of equals, beside Contracts, which is trivia.
  • The add a vehicle form is permanently open above the fleet, so the rarest job takes the most valuable space. The create account form does the same thing lower down.
  • No search. Finding a plate or a customer means reading every row.
  • Nothing shows what is due back today, which is the question the phone is ringing about.
What is already right
  • The data behind it is good: /api/admin/overview already returns every car in every status, open rentals with customer and dates, contract counts and the latest contract date.
  • Every refusal is already modelled properly — duplicate-plate, has-history, last-owner, status-change-refused, username-taken.
  • English and German are both complete, and the strings below are the real ones.
None of the three options needs a new API

All of them are built from what /api/admin/overview and the accounts endpoints already return. The work is in the front end — plus splitting the 1,017-line AdminDashboard.tsx, which is currently sign-in, counters, fleet, rentals and account management in one file.

Good at
  • The blocked car is impossible to miss — first thing on the page, with the reason and the button on the same row.
  • An empty band is a real signal: nothing to do today.
  • Smallest change from what exists. The queue is derived from data the overview endpoint already returns.
Costs
  • Weak when the job is look something up — search is present but secondary.
  • The queue has to be genuinely reliable. A dashboard saying "nothing to do" while a return sits unconfirmed is worse than today's.
  • Fleet editing and accounts both move behind a click, which is slower for the rare admin session.

What the form refuses

That username is already taken. At least 10 characters. This is the last owner. Make somebody else an owner first.
What this fixes
  • Staff never see this screen. Today the section is hidden but the distinction isn't visible — here, a staff member gets only the My password card, which is theirs by right.
  • The last owner is explained, not just refused. The row says why the buttons are absent instead of failing with 409 last-owner after the click.
  • A disabled account looks disabled in the list, rather than being a state you discover by trying to use it.
  • Last sign-in becomes useful — never next to a real date is how you spot an account that was created and forgotten.
Already built behind it
  • POST /api/admin/users and PATCH /api/admin/users/[id], with username-taken, last-owner, forbidden and not-found all distinguished.
  • Any signed-in person can change their own password; only an owner sees the rest.
  • Disabling takes effect on that person's very next click, and a password change ends their other sessions.

What I'd do

Recommendation — A now, C later

Option A, built properly, and structured so it becomes C without a rewrite. The office has nine cars and three accounts. A seven-item rail is furniture the business does not have the weight for yet, and Option B optimises for the wrong failure: the expensive mistake here is not a slow lookup, it is a car sitting blocked because nobody confirmed a return — the one thing today's screen actively hides.

Build the queue as its own component over its own selector, and accounts as its own route from the first commit. When Customers and Contracts earn their own screens, the rail gets added around them and the queue becomes the Overview pane. No throwaway work.

Included either way

Splitting components/admin/AdminDashboard.tsx. One 1,017-line file currently holds the sign-in card, the counters, the fleet list, the add-car form, the rentals list, the accounts table, the create-account form and the change-my-password control. Every option above needs it broken into a shell plus one component per section — and both label sets stay exactly as they are, since they are already complete.

Nothing here touches the pickup or return wizards. Those are a separate job with a different audience, and worth doing after this lands — alongside the per-rental pickup link, which replaces the shared APPLY_SECRET with a button on this dashboard.
