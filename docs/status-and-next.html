Where We Stand

Eleven commits, 404 tests. The fleet console is built, retention is enforced and the documents are reachable. Three things turned out to be actually broken along the way, and the worst of them was losing money quietly.

The three that were broken

None of these were on the list. All three were found by checking a claim rather than trusting it, and all three are fixed.

1

No car added through /admin could ever be returned

The return form rendered the hard-coded list of nine cars in lib/rental/fleet.ts and never called /api/fleet. Because persistReturn finds the rental from the car, a car that could not be picked could not be returned — the rental had no reachable way to end.

Reported as "I don't see the Mercedes I added". It was not a display bug. Nine static entries hid it: with the seeded fleet the picker looked plausible. /api/fleet?scope=return now answers with the cars that are actually out.

2

The office confirmed returns blind — and most answers were never stored

Cleanliness, papers, key, fines, payment method, amount paid, the outstanding balance and the deposit had been collected and validated since the return form existed, and written only into the PDF. The office was asked to press the button that frees the car without being able to read any of it.

Fifteen columns now exist, and a submitted return opens a review modal instead of a plain confirm button.

3

Money a customer admitted owing was never chased

Charges were raised by pickup and by extensions — never by a return. So "I still owe CHF 200, due the 30th" was a sentence inside a document in a bucket. No Charge row, so the scheduler never reminded anybody and the office never got an alert. The money depended on somebody remembering.

Confirming a return now raises a charge the existing scheduler chases. On confirmation rather than submission, so nobody is chased over a number a customer typed and no one checked.

Also fixed, on request
  • The pickup link lost its shared key — zuriauto.ch/pickup/, nothing to paste after it.
  • A reveal toggle on every password field, including when setting somebody else's.
  • The 1,017-line dashboard is a routed console: sidebar, mobile bar, dialogs, readable tables.

What is worth doing next

Ranked by what it costs the business to leave alone, not by how hard it is.

✓
done

Retention is now enforced

lib/admin/retention.ts runs as the last pass of the daily cron: person-assets whose rental ended more than five years ago have their bytes deleted and the row stamped. The contract PDF and the records are untouched — ten years under OR 958f.

Correction to an earlier draft of this page, which said 90 days for identity images and two years for condition photographs. Those were the superseded proposal; DATA-RETENTION.md records that the owner agreed five and ten years on 22 August 2026, and the code follows the agreed figures.

✓
done

Documents are in the dashboard

The signed contracts, ID scans, licences, condition photos and signatures are reachable only through the Cloudflare console. When a traffic fine arrives and you need the contract plus the driver's licence for that date, that is where you go.

A Documents button on every rental. The bytes are proxied through the server rather than handed out as presigned URLs — a presigned URL is a bearer token for somebody's passport that survives being forwarded and cannot be audited once issued. Every view writes an AssetAccess row naming who asked.

The office cannot see what is owed

Charges exist, the scheduler chases them, reminders go out — and there is no screen anywhere showing the total outstanding, who owes it, or what is overdue. The system knows; the office does not.

2
moneysmaller

Damage notes have no photographs

DAMAGE_PHOTO is in the schema enum and nothing has ever written it. The return form takes a damage note and no picture.

A sentence is not evidence when a customer disputes a charge. The pickup form already photographs condition; the return is the half that matters for billing.

3
daily frictionsmaller

Extension requests arrive nowhere

A customer can request an extension from the link in their reminder email. EXTENSION_REQUESTED is a real rental status. No admin screen shows the request or lets anybody accept it.

4
daily frictionbigger

The customer side of the recall feature

Recognising a returning customer by phone number works — it fills the pickup form and carries their documents forward. But the office cannot search it: no way to ask is this person a customer, how many rentals, any damage history, do we hold their ID?

The phone key is already indexed. Open question from the spec: a Customers list, or just a search box? A ringing phone wants search.

5
securitysmaller

Per-rental pickup links

The shared key is gone, which is what was asked for. The stronger version costs less friction, not more: the office presses New rental and gets a single-use expiring link for that customer. The machinery exists — ActionToken already does this for reminder emails.

Before production

Separate from the list above. These are not features.

Must happen first

  • Rotate the chef password. It was generated during setup and written into a chat transcript.
  • Add ADMIN_OWNER_USERNAME and ADMIN_OWNER_PASSWORD to .env.preview.template and scripts/set-preview-env.sh. Without them the next fresh database seeds no owner and refuses every sign-in with no explanation on screen.
  • Follow docs/DEPLOY-PHASE-2-3.md. Production has never been deployed; neondb is empty. Steps 1 and 2 are irreversible.
The schema window is open, and closing

Because production has no data, a migration today costs one command. The return protocol columns were added now for exactly that reason. Anything else structural — retention fields, damage photos, a customers index — is cheapest before the office starts using it for real.
