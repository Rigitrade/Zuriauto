Suite A · 6 drills

The fences hold

The pickup form used to be fenced with a shared office key. It is not any more — that was removed so the office could hand out a bare link. These drills prove what replaced it actually holds, and that the fleet page is still a different story.

A1

The pickup form opens for anyone

Open /pickup/ with nothing after it, in a private window.

Expect

The five-step wizard. No Link not valid screen — that gate is gone, and its strings were deleted from both languages.

This is the change itself, so confirm it first. Then open an old /apply/?k=… link: it should redirect to /pickup/ and work, with the stale key ignored rather than refused, so nothing already sent over WhatsApp breaks.

A2

A cross-site post is still refused

From the console on any other site, post to https://<preview>/api/rental-contract/.

Expect

403 bad-origin. With the key gone, this and the rate limiter are what stand between the form and a script.

An absent Origin header is allowed on purpose, for native clients that do not send one — so this stops a browser-driven attack, not a determined one with curl. The limiter in E7 is the backstop that does.

A3

APPLY_SECRET still does its one remaining job

Run a pickup for a customer who has rented before (drill E6). Then unset APPLY_SECRET in the Preview environment, redeploy, and try again.

Expect

With it set, their stored ID and licence are offered for reuse. With it unset, that step fails with a 500 and no helpful message — while everything else on the form still works, which is exactly why it is easy to miss.

It fences nothing any more; it signs the short-lived document-reuse token in lib/rental/reuseToken.ts. It must stay set in every environment, and must still never equal ADMIN_SECRET — scripts/set-preview-env.sh refuses to push an environment where the two are equal. Put it back before carrying on.

A4

The fleet page with no session

Open /admin/ in a private window.

Expect

The sign-in card only. No counters, no car list, no rental data sitting behind it in the page source.

A5

Nothing links to the admin page

Open / and read the header, the footer and the page source. Then check /sitemap.xml.

Expect

No link to /admin anywhere, and index: false, follow: false in its metadata.

Obscurity is not the fence — the password is. But a staff tool that turns up in a search result invites someone to start guessing at it.

A6

Health gives away nothing worth having

Open /api/health/ while signed out and read it as an attacker would.

Expect

Booleans, never values. Every secret it names fails closed when unset, so knowing which are missing tells you only that the doors are locked.

Suite B · 7 drills

Sign-in and sessions

One answer for every kind of refusal, and a cookie that can be revoked despite being stateless. B7 is the one that surprises people.

B1

Right name, wrong password

Sign in as chef with wrongwrongwrong.

Expect

One short refusal, with no hint that the username was the half that was right.

B2

A username that does not exist

Sign in as nobody with anything. Watch the network tab's timing column.

Expect

The same message as B1, and — the part worth measuring — in roughly the same time.

The route verifies a real hash of a value nobody knows when no user matched. Without it, an unknown name would return in a millisecond while a known one paid the scrypt cost, turning the login into a way to enumerate who works here.

B3

The session survives a reload

Sign in, reload, close the tab, reopen /admin/.

Expect

Still signed in, still showing your display name. The cookie is good for twelve hours — one working day, so the office signs in once each morning.

B4

Signing out actually signs out

Sign out, then press the browser's Back button.

Expect

The sign-in card — not a cached dashboard with live figures still on it.

B5

Eleven wrong passwords in a rowlocks you out

Submit a wrong password eleven times without pausing.

Expect

The first ten refuse normally. The eleventh returns 429 and stays refused for ten minutes — from your address only.

Do this last, and only once

You are locking your own address out of /admin for ten minutes. A phone on mobile data is a second address if you need back in sooner.

Ten failures per ten minutes, and only failures are charged. The reason is in the next drill.

B6

A busy morning is not an attack

Sign in and out five times in a row, correctly, in under a minute.

Expect

No rate limiting at all.

The office sits behind one address with up to ten accounts. If correct sign-ins were charged, ten people arriving at nine o'clock would lock the desk out of its own dashboard.

B7

Changing your password ends your other sessionswrites

Sign in on a laptop and a phone. On the laptop, change your own password. Then click something on the phone.

Expect

The phone is signed out on its very next click — not twelve hours later when the cookie would have expired.

The cookie carries the moment it was issued, and every request compares that to credentialsChangedAt. It is what lets a stateless cookie still be revoked, and it is exactly what you want if the reason for the reset is that somebody else knew the old password.

Suite C · 7 drills

Accounts

Two roles, and a set of refusals that exist so the office cannot lock itself out of its own system.

C1

Create a staff accountwrites

As chef, add a second account with role staff.

Expect

Created, and listed. Keep the password — C3 and C4 need it.

C2

The names the form refuses

Try to create accounts called, in turn:

Chef          an uppercase letter
ab            two characters
mo hamed      a space
schwätzer     a non-ASCII letter
chef          already taken

Then one valid name with the password kurz.

Expect

All six refused, each naming the field that was wrong. The rule is ^[a-z0-9._-]{3,32}$; passwords are 10–200 characters. The duplicate gets its own answer — username taken, 409 — rather than being lumped in with the malformed ones.

The same schema runs in the seed, so a malformed ADMIN_OWNER_USERNAME fails loudly at seed time instead of creating an account nobody can sign into.

C3

Staff cannot manage accounts

Sign in as the staff account from C1 and look for the accounts section.

Expect

It is not there, and the API behind it answers 403 forbidden — a different code from 401 unauthorised, because "signed in but not allowed" and "not signed in" are genuinely different problems.

C4

Staff can still change their own passwordwrites

Still signed in as staff, change your own password, then sign in again with the new one.

Expect

Allowed, signed out immediately afterwards, and the new password works. Every account can rotate its own credential without asking an owner to do it.

C5

The last owner cannot stand down

As chef — the only owner — try to change your own role to staff, then try to disable yourself.

Expect

Both refused, 409 last-owner. An organisation with no owner has nobody left who can create one.

C6

A disabled account stops working mid-sessionwrites

Sign in as staff on a phone. On the laptop, as chef, disable that account. Click something on the phone.

Expect

Refused on the next click, without waiting for the cookie to expire.

Every request reads the account row, which is what buys revocation a stateless cookie cannot have on its own. One indexed read, against a dashboard serving a handful of requests a day.

C7

The way back in from the command linewrites

With DATABASE_URL pointed at the preview branch:

pnpm admin:password chef 'EinNeuesPasswort2026'

Then try the same against an account that does not exist.

Expect

The first succeeds and reports that other sessions were ended. The second refuses — this script resets an existing password and cannot create a missing account. That distinction is why a forgotten owner password is recoverable and a missing owner row is not.

Suite D · 7 drills

The fleet

Identity lives in code, status lives in the database. The status whitelist is the interesting part: everything involving rented is refused by omission.

D1

Add a carwrites

Add the BMW you originally came here for — model, plate, VIN.

Expect

Created available, and the counter goes from 9 to 10.

D2

The same plate twice

Add another car carrying the plate you just used.

Expect

Refused, 409 duplicate-plate. A plate is a legal identifier printed on a signed contract; two cars cannot share one.

D3

Edit, then edit into a collisionwrites

Change the BMW's model and VIN. Then change its plate to one an existing car already carries.

Expect

The first saves. The second is refused with the same duplicate-plate — the rule holds on update, not only on create.

D4

Retire and un-retirewrites

Set a car to retired, then back to available. Then submit the form again without changing anything.

Expect

All three allowed. Those two transitions plus the no-ops are the entire whitelist, and the no-ops are there so a resubmitted form is not an error.

D5

A retired car is not on offer

Retire a car, then open the public fleet page and the pickup form's car list.

Expect

Gone from both. Retiring is how a car comes off the road without a deploy.

D6

Nobody can hand-set a car to rented

There is no control for this in the UI, which is the point — so it is an API check. In the browser console on /admin/, where your session cookie rides along automatically: 

await (await fetch('/api/admin/cars/<id>/', {
  method: 'PATCH',
  headers: { 'content-type': 'application/json' },
  body: JSON.stringify({ status: 'rented' })
})).json()
Expect

{ code: "status-change-refused", from: "available", to: "rented" }

A whitelist rather than a blacklist, so adding a status to the enum later cannot silently make a new transition reachable. rented belongs to the rental lifecycle, never to a form.

D7

A car with history cannot be deleted

Come back to this after suite F. Try to delete the car you rented out — then delete the BMW from D1, which has no history.

Expect

The first refused, 409 has-history. The second deleted. Retiring is the correct move for any car that has ever been on a contract; deleting it would orphan a ten-year commercial record.

Suite E · 8 drills

One rental, and everything that can go wrong with it

This is where the preview writes real data and sends real mail. E1 is the point of no return for the office's inbox if MAIL_OFFICE is still the real address.

E1

A complete pickupwritessends mail

Open the pickup link with the key and go all the way through: your own name and email, a car, dates, ID and licence images, condition photos, signature.

Expect
  • A contract number, and a PDF that opens
  • Mail at the renter address — yours
  • The car's status is now rented
  • /admin/ shows one open rental, with the customer and the dates
E2

The same car, twice over

Start a second pickup and try to choose the car you just rented out.

Expect

Not offered. A car on an open contract cannot be handed to a second driver.

E3

An incomplete contract

Start again and try to advance each step with the required field empty — no name, no email, no dates, no signature. Then set an end date before the start date.

Expect

Every step blocks, naming the field. Nothing reaches the database.

E4

A PDF that is too big, and one with too many pages

Where the form takes a PDF, offer one over 1.5 MB, then one with more than five pages, then a .jpg renamed to .pdf.

Expect

All three refused before anything is uploaded. The page count is checked by reading the file, not by trusting its name or its size.

E5

Photographs, from an actual phone

Open the pickup link on a phone and use the camera for the ID and the condition photos. Then decline the camera permission and use the file picker instead.

Expect

Both work. Declining the camera gives No camera found. Please choose a file instead. rather than a dead end.

This is the real device story — the office does this at the kerbside, not at a desk.

E6

A returning customer does not re-scan their passportwrites

Start a third pickup using the same name and email as E1.

Expect

The existing customer is found, and their stored documents are offered for reuse rather than demanded again.

E7

Six pickups in ten minutes

Submit the pickup form six times in quick succession.

Expect

The sixth is rate-limited — five per ten minutes per address. It has its own budget, so spending it here does not lock you out of /admin, and B5 does not lock you out of here.

E8

The images really are in the EU

wrangler r2 bucket list

Find the bucket holding what you just uploaded and read its jurisdiction.

Expect

EU. A bucket's jurisdiction is fixed at creation, and this one holds ID scans and driving licences.

Suite F · 5 drills

The return

/return/ has no key at all — it is a public page, because the renter holding the phone has no credential anyone can rely on. What protects it instead: the rental is found from the car and never supplied by the caller, and submitting it deliberately does not free the car.

F1

Return the carwritessends mail

Open /return/ — no key, nothing after it — pick the car you rented out in E1, and fill in mileage, fuel level and any damage photos.

Expect
  • A confirmation naming the distance driven
  • The rental is now RETURN_SUBMITTED
  • The car is still rented
  • /admin/ badges it Zurückgegeben – bestätigen

The car is not freed here on purpose. /return/ is unfenced, so letting it put a car back on the road would let anyone who can read a numberplate return a car somebody is still driving — and the next customer could then sign a contract for it.

F2

The form is open to anyone, and that is the design

Open /return/ in a private window, from a phone on mobile data, having never seen an email. Then look at what the page is willing to tell you about a car before you submit anything.

Expect

The form loads for anybody. What it must not do is confirm who is renting a given car, or let a stranger's submission change what /admin shows as available — that is F4's job.

The guards here are not a key: a same-origin check, a per-address rate limit, a honeypot field, PDF-only with a size cap, and the rule that matters most — the rental is looked up from the car, never taken from the request. A caller cannot name someone else's rental id.

F3

The same car, returned twice

Submit a second return for the car you just returned in F1, before the office has confirmed anything.

Expect

The rental is still found — deliberate — so you get an honest already returned rather than the misleading no rental is out on this car, which reads as though the first return was never recorded. The write is refused: it is guarded on ACTIVE and EXTENSION_REQUESTED only.

Also try a car nobody is driving. That one genuinely has no open rental, and should say so.

F4

The office confirms, and the car comes backwrites

On /admin/, confirm the return.

Expect

The rental moves to COMPLETED and the car back to available, with the counters moving to match. This is the one click that frees a car.

F5

Closing a rental that is already closed

Confirm the same return a second time — the button again, or a repeated request.

Expect

409 already-closed, naming the status it is already in. A double click does not double-free a car.

Suite G · 4 drills

The daily run

Vercel Cron does not fire on preview deployments, so a manual trigger is the only way to exercise any of this — and that needs the bypass token from the top of this page.

G1

The scheduler refuses an unsigned call

curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}\n' \
  -H "x-vercel-protection-bypass: $BYPASS" \
  $PREVIEW_URL/api/cron/daily/
Expect

401. Without the bypass header you get 302 instead, which tells you nothing at all about the fence.

G2

A wrong bearer token

curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}\n' \
  -H "x-vercel-protection-bypass: $BYPASS" \
  -H "authorization: Bearer wrong" \
  $PREVIEW_URL/api/cron/daily/
Expect

401 — the same answer as no token at all.

G3

The real daily runwritessends mail

curl -s -H "x-vercel-protection-bypass: $BYPASS" \
  -H "authorization: Bearer $CRON_SECRET" \
  $PREVIEW_URL/api/cron/daily/ | jq
Expect

A summary of what it did. With a rental ending inside the next 48 hours, a reminder goes out — to your address, because you used it as the renter's.

The window is 48 hours wide rather than 24 because the free plan fires the cron once a day, within the hour, rather than at a precise time.

G4

A returned rental is still chased for money

Run G3 again after F1 but before F4 — so the rental sits at RETURN_SUBMITTED with a charge outstanding.

Expect

The charge is still picked up. Handing the keys back does not settle the bill.

Filtering the scheduler on ACTIVE alone would quietly stop chasing money that is still owed — the exact bug fixed in 65d3f0b, and the drill most worth keeping when the rest of this page has gone stale.

Suite H · 3 drills

Language, device, and interruption

H1

Both languages, all the way through

Run a pickup in German and one in English, reading the sign-in card, the error messages and the generated PDF in each.

Expect

No English leaking into a German contract, and no untranslated key anywhere. The legal terms are the part to read properly rather than skim.

H2

A slow connection, then a lost one

With the network throttled to Slow 3G, submit a pickup. Then, mid-upload, switch to Offline.

Expect

A visible pending state rather than a frozen button, and an error you can act on rather than a silent failure. Afterwards, check whether a half-finished rental was left behind.

H3

Reload in the middle of the wizard

Get as far as the signature step, reload the page, and see what survives.

Expect

Whatever it does, it should be honest about it — either the work is still there, or it clearly starts again. What it must not do is submit a contract with half the fields missing.

Afterwards

The preview database now holds real-shaped test data: customers, contracts, and images in the EU bucket. Part 6 of the release test run has the cleanup, and docs/DATA-RETENTION.md has the rules that apply to the images specifically. Delete the test customers' documents rather than leaving them in the bucket — if you used your own ID, as instructed, they are scans of a real person's papers.

Two known gaps — not bugs to file
  • ADMIN_OWNER_USERNAME and ADMIN_OWNER_PASSWORD are missing from .env.preview.template and from the required list in scripts/set-preview-env.sh, so the next fresh database seeds with no owner and refuses every sign-in with no explanation on screen.
  • The owner password now in use was generated during setup and written into a chat transcript. Rotate it with C7 before this account guards anything real.
